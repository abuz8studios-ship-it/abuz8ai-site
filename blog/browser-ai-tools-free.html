How the studios work — one paragraph, no magic

Each studio page looks for a ComfyUI engine at your computer's standard local address when it loads. Found: the Generate button goes live, and the browser submits a pre-built, pre-tested workflow to your engine over your machine's own internal connection — the render never touches the internet. Not found: the page says exactly that, tells you what is missing, and offers the early-access list for the managed version instead. Every workflow behind these pages was submitted to a live engine and verified to produce real output before the page shipped — a studio either works or says it does not.

Why this architecture instead of a normal web app

Because the normal architecture has a normal failure mode: the server bill arrives, the free tier shrinks, the watermark appears, and your uploaded files sit in a retention policy. A tool that runs on your hardware cannot be metered, cannot be throttled, and cannot leak what it never received. That is the position argued in local AI vs. cloud AI, and the studios are the proof-of-work: not a whitepaper about sovereignty, six pages you can use before lunch.
