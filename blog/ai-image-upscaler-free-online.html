How AI Upscaling Actually Works

Traditional upscaling algorithms (Lanczos, bicubic) work by interpolating — essentially guessing what color to put between existing pixels based on their neighbors. The result is mathematically smooth but visually blurry because there's no actual new information.

AI upscaling models — specifically Real-ESRGAN and its derivatives — work differently. They were trained on millions of image pairs (low-res and high-res versions of the same image). The model learned that when it sees a certain texture pattern at low resolution, that pattern typically looks like X at 4x resolution. It applies that learned knowledge to reconstruct detail that wasn't in the original file.

When to Use Which Upscaling Factor

Not every image needs 4x upscaling. Here's how to choose:

  • 2x upscale: Good source image (1080p), just needs a small size boost for web or light print use (8x10 at 150dpi)
  • 4x upscale: Smaller source image (720p or less), needs to be displayed large (banner, poster, large format print)
  • 8x+ upscale: Usually produces artifacts even with AI. Better to 4x upscale, then run through a sharpening pass. ABUZ8 does this automatically.

Common Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

AI upscaling is powerful but not magic. People ruin their results by making these mistakes:

  1. Upscaling already compressed images without pre-processing: If your source image has heavy JPEG compression artifacts, the AI will upscale those artifacts along with the detail. Run a denoising pass first (ABUZ8's upscaler does this automatically).
  2. Expecting blurry photos to become sharp: AI upscaling adds resolution, not focus. A blurry photo stays blurry at 4x — just larger. Motion blur or focus blur cannot be corrected by upscaling alone.
  3. Upscaling screenshots: Text in screenshots often looks weird after AI upscaling because the model treats it as texture. For screenshots with text, standard bicubic interpolation or vector conversion is better.
