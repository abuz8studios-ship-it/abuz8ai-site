The Best Fireflies Alternative in 2026 (No Bot in Your Meetings)

COMPARISONJUL 7, 20267 MIN READ

Most people searching for a Fireflies alternative want the same result — automatic transcripts, summaries, and action items from their calls — without the part they’ve started to dread: a third-party bot joining the meeting and streaming everyone’s audio up to a vendor’s cloud. In 2026 the better answer is a meeting assistant that does all the note-taking on your own machine, so the recording never leaves your building in the first place.

What people are actually trying to escape

Notetaker tools are genuinely useful, which is why the frustration is so specific. There’s the awkward bot that shows up in the participant list and makes clients ask “who is Fireflies?” There’s the per-seat pricing that climbs as your team grows. There are the storage limits and the upsell to unlock more. And underneath it all: your sales calls, strategy sessions, and one-on-ones are being uploaded, transcribed, and retained on infrastructure you don’t control. For regulated work — legal, health, finance — that upload is a compliance question, not a convenience.

What a real alternative needs to do

Feature parity is the easy bar: accurate transcription, a clean summary, extracted action items and decisions, and something searchable afterward. The higher bar is where the work happens. A genuine alternative processes the audio locally — no bot in the call, no upload, no third-party retention — and still hands you the transcript and summary you came for. If it can do that on your own hardware, unlimited, for free after setup, you’ve fixed the pricing and the privacy problem at the same time.

The bot test: a lot of “alternatives” still send a bot to join and record. If your goal is privacy, that’s the same architecture with a new name. The real shift is recording locally and transcribing on your own machine — nothing joins, nothing uploads.

How a local meeting agent works

The pieces are all open and mature now. Local speech-to-text turns the audio into an accurate transcript on your GPU or even a laptop CPU — the same technology we cover in how to run Whisper locally. A local language model then reads that transcript and produces the summary, the action items, and the follow-up email draft. Nothing in that chain calls a vendor. The result is the Fireflies outcome — searchable notes and next steps in your inbox — produced by a pipeline that runs entirely on your side of the wire.

It fits the way you already meet

None of this asks you to change how you run calls. Record your video meeting the way you already do, drop the audio file in, and the local pipeline does the rest — transcript, summary, action items. Same for an in-person conversation caught on your phone, or a sales call recorded with consent. There’s no bot to add to the invite, no calendar integration to grant, and no account for the other side to wonder about. The assistant sits at the end of your existing workflow instead of inserting itself into the middle of it, which is exactly why it’s easy to adopt.

Notes that turn into work

The point of meeting notes isn’t the notes; it’s what happens next. Because ABUZ8 OS runs an agent alongside the transcription, the summary doesn’t just sit in a folder. It can draft the recap email, add the action items to your list, and pull the decisions into a running record you can search months later. Our writeups on AI meeting notes and the AI meeting summarizer go deeper on turning a raw transcript into something your team actually uses — and the whole flow stays on your machine.

“But is local transcription actually accurate?”

Fair question, and the honest answer is: for most business audio, yes. The larger local speech-to-text models are competitive with the cloud services on clear English calls, and they pull ahead on anything you can control — a decent microphone, one speaker at a time, low background noise. Where cloud tools still hold an edge is heavy accents, people talking over each other, and rare languages, because they run the biggest models on the biggest hardware. The practical move: run a large local model for the accuracy, and accept that the occasional messy call might need a manual tidy-up. For the privacy and the zero per-minute cost, that’s a trade most teams take happily.

Why local matters more for meetings than almost anything

A meeting is the single most sensitive stream of text most businesses produce — deal terms, personnel decisions, roadmaps, client confidences — and it’s exactly the content you least want on a third-party server. That’s the same logic behind running a private ChatGPT for your business: the more valuable the words, the more the “just upload it” default costs you. A local meeting assistant keeps your most confidential conversations where they belong.

The bottom line

The best Fireflies alternative in 2026 isn’t a cheaper bot — it’s no bot at all. Record locally, transcribe on your own hardware, summarize with a local model, and get your notes and action items without a single byte leaving your building. You keep the outcome, drop the per-seat bill, and stop uploading your most sensitive conversations to someone else’s cloud. That’s the trade worth making.
