A face is not just another image

Worth saying directly: of everything people push through AI tools, faces are the most personal. A cartoon avatar app that uploads your selfies is collecting biometric source material, whatever its privacy page says. Running the cartoonization locally is not a style preference — it is the difference between making an avatar and donating your face. This studio's renders never leave your machine; the browser talks to your own engine over your computer's internal connection, and our site's only role is serving the page.

What the studio actually does

The page drives a cartoon-tuned diffusion workflow on your ComfyUI engine. Describe the avatar — "smiling bearded guy, round glasses, flat colors, thick outlines" — pick your settings, and generate. Each run pulls a fresh seed, so you can spin variations until one feels like you. Flat-color, thick-outline styles read best at 128 pixels, which is how your avatar will actually be seen; save the painterly detail for full-size art from the anime studio, its sibling on the same engine.

Small-canvas rule: an avatar lives its whole life as a thumbnail. High contrast between hair and background, one accessory at most, and a face that fills two-thirds of the frame will out-perform an intricate masterpiece every time. Generate five, shrink them all to icon size, and pick the one that still reads.

Consistency, if you want a recurring character

One render makes an avatar; a repeatable character makes a brand. Streamers and newsletter writers often want the same cartoon face in different poses across months of content. The path there is seed discipline and prompt reuse — keep the descriptive core identical and vary only the pose and expression clauses. It is not perfect identity lock, but on a cartoon style it is remarkably close, and iterating costs you nothing locally, which is the entire advantage over per-render pricing.
