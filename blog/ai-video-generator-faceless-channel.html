The pipeline, end to end

Script → Clips → Voiceover → Assembly → Thumbnail → Title. Six stages. AI does five of them well. The one it can't do is the one that decides everything: knowing what your audience actually wants next.

Script: write or AI-draft a tight script — for shorts, 60–90 words; for long-form, an outline with a hook every 45 seconds. Clips: generate scene-by-scene with text-to-video, one prompt per beat of the script, keeping a consistent style token across all prompts so the video doesn't feel like a slideshow of different artists. Voiceover: TTS has crossed the believability line for documentary-style narration; keep it slightly slower than feels natural. Assembly: cut clips to the narration, not the other way around. Thumbnail: our thumbnail maker — high contrast, one focal object, three words max.

Niches where faceless works (and why)

Faceless wins where the subject is the star: explainers and "how X works," history and true stories, ambient and background content, data stories and rankings, and niche deep-dives (machines, space, oceans, money). It loses where the person is the star: vlogs, reactions, opinion commentary. If a niche's top channels all show faces, the audience is buying a human relationship — don't fight that with clips.

The retention trap that kills AI channels

Platforms don't punish AI video; they punish boring video, and lazy AI video is reliably boring. The failure pattern: one static generated shot per 15 seconds of narration. Viewers feel the wallpaper effect and swipe. The fix is cut density — change the visual every 3–5 seconds, mix generated clips with motion (pans, zooms), and put your single best visual in the first two seconds, not after the intro. Generate 3x more clips than your runtime needs and cut ruthlessly. Generation is free; retention isn't.

Consistency is the moat

One good video is luck; a channel is a look. Lock a style phrase into every prompt ("cinematic, muted teal-and-amber grade, shallow depth of field") and reuse it for every video. Use the same voice, same music energy, same thumbnail layout. Viewers should recognize your video with the sound off. This is also where our consistent character generator earns its slot — a recurring visual mascot gives a faceless channel a face that isn't yours.

The math nobody puts in the thumbnail

Expect the first 20–30 videos to earn approximately nothing — that's the platform learning who your content is for. The channels that break through post on a fixed cadence for months, study which of their own videos over-performed, and double down. AI's real gift is that it makes this survivable: when a video takes 40 minutes instead of 8 hours, you can afford the 30-video apprenticeship.

Disclosure and rights, quickly

Platforms now ask you to flag realistic AI-generated content — flag it; the checkbox costs nothing and a strike costs the channel. Don't generate real people. Use music you have rights to (our AI music generator exists precisely so your background track can't get claimed).
