Here's what a working signature actually contains, the mistakes that quietly hurt deliverability, and how to build a clean one in a minute with our free AI email signature generator.

What belongs in a signature (it's less than you think)

The working formula is four lines: name, role and company, one way to reach you beyond reply, and one link — the single place you most want people to go. That's it. Every element you add past that point dilutes the others. If your signature offers a website, a calendar link, five social icons, a phone number, a fax number, and a banner, you've built a tiny link farm nobody clicks. Pick the one action that matters for your business — book a call, see the product, read the blog — and give it the only link slot.

The mistakes that cost more than they look

Image-only signatures. A signature that's one big picture looks fine on your screen and fails everywhere else: images are blocked by default in many clients, invisible in plain-text mode, unreadable to screen readers, and their text can't be copied. Your phone number should be selectable text, not pixels. Oversized logos. A 2 MB logo attached to every message bloats threads and can nudge spam scoring — email filters weigh the ratio of image to text. Inspirational quotes and legal walls. The quote ages badly; the six-paragraph disclaimer gets ignored and makes short emails look absurd below it. If your industry genuinely requires a disclaimer, keep it to the required sentence, small and gray. Reply-chain stacking. Your full signature belongs on the first message; replies need at most your name. Ten signatures stacked in one thread is how a two-line conversation becomes a scroll.

Design rules that survive every inbox

Email clients render HTML inconsistently, so the durable signatures are typographically boring on purpose: system-safe fonts, one accent color, no more than two font sizes, and structure built with simple layout rather than fragile positioning. Dark-mode inboxes will invert or dim your colors — mid-tone accents survive; pale grays vanish. Test the result three places before adopting it: your phone, a webmail client, and plain-text view. If the signature still communicates in all three, ship it.

One signature, or several?

People forget you can run more than one. A sales conversation benefits from the calendar-link version; a support reply benefits from the docs-link version; an investor email might carry the one-line company descriptor. The generator makes producing a small set trivial — same identity, different single link. Pair it with the AI bio writer if the "who are you" line itself is what's stalling you, and with the email subject tester for the other end of the message — the line that decides whether the email gets opened at all.

The two-minute setup

Generate, copy, then paste into your client's signature settings: Gmail under Settings → General → Signature, Outlook under Settings → Compose → Signatures, Apple Mail under Settings → Signatures. Set the full version as default for new messages and the name-only version for replies. Then leave it alone for a year — a good signature is infrastructure, not a hobby.
