The Best Glean Alternative in 2026 (Work Search You Self-Host)

COMPARISONJUL 7, 20268 MIN READ

Teams evaluating a Glean alternative tend to agree on the goal and disagree on the trade: they want an AI that can search across everything the company knows and answer questions in plain language — but they’re increasingly unwilling to pipe every internal document, message, and file through a vendor’s cloud to get it. In 2026 the alternative worth taking seriously is work search that runs on infrastructure you control, so your knowledge stays inside your walls.

What “work AI” actually promises

The pitch is compelling: connect your docs, wikis, tickets, and chat, and get a single assistant that can answer “what did we decide about pricing in Q1?” or “where’s the onboarding checklist?” by reading your own material. It works because of retrieval — the system indexes your content and pulls the relevant passages before answering. That’s real value. The catch is that delivering it usually means granting a third party standing access to the most sensitive corpus you have: everything your company writes down.

Why the data question dominates

Consumer chatbots leak a prompt at a time. A work-search tool is different in kind — it’s designed to ingest all of it: contracts, roadmaps, HR files, source code, customer records. The convenience scales with the sensitivity. So the central question for any alternative isn’t “how good are the answers” — it’s “where does our entire knowledge base live, and who else can touch it?” A self-hosted answer changes that from a vendor-trust exercise into something you administer yourself.

The architecture is the security model. If the index of your company’s knowledge sits on a vendor’s servers, no policy page changes the fact that it’s there. Self-hosting isn’t a feature checkbox — it’s the difference between trusting a promise and controlling the machine.

How a self-hosted work assistant works

The building blocks are the same ones powering every serious AI assistant, just kept on your side. Your documents are chunked and stored in a search index — the job of a vector database. When someone asks a question, the system retrieves the most relevant passages and hands them to a language model to answer from, the pattern we explain in RAG explained for AI agents. Add persistent agent memory so it remembers context across sessions, and run every piece — index, retrieval, model — on your own hardware. Same capability as the hosted version; none of the data handoff.

Who needs this most

Any team can use work search, but the ones who truly need the self-hosted version are the ones whose knowledge is the business. Law firms and their privileged files. Healthcare and finance under strict data rules. Product and engineering orgs whose docs and code are the crown jewels. Agencies holding many clients’ confidences at once. For all of them the hosted convenience isn’t worth the exposure — and a local assistant that reads everything while sharing nothing is the only version that clears the bar.

The questions it answers

The value lands in the everyday questions that normally cost someone twenty minutes of digging. “Where’s the latest security policy?” “What did we tell this customer back in March?” “Which doc has the current onboarding steps?” “What did we actually decide about pricing last quarter?” A good work assistant reads across your wikis, tickets, docs, and threads and answers in plain language, with a pointer to the source so you can trust it. Multiply that by every employee, every day, and the time saved is real — which is precisely why you want that capability without renting out the knowledge base it’s reading from.

Search that becomes action

Finding the answer is step one. Because ABUZ8 OS runs an agent, not just a search box, the assistant can act on what it finds — draft the reply, compile the summary, kick off the follow-up — while staying entirely local. It’s the same private-first logic behind running a private ChatGPT for your business, extended across your whole knowledge base instead of a single chat. For teams in regulated fields, that combination — powerful internal search plus zero external data exposure — is the only version that survives a security review.

How you actually roll it out

Self-hosted work search doesn’t have to be a big-bang migration, and it shouldn’t be. The sane path starts narrow: point it at one team’s documents — support’s knowledge base, engineering’s docs, a single shared drive — and prove the answers are good before you widen it. Because everything runs on your own hardware, expanding is a matter of adding sources to the index, not renegotiating a per-seat contract. The honest trade versus a hosted tool: hosted platforms ship with a long list of pre-built connectors, so if you want a dozen SaaS apps wired in on day one, that’s their strength. Self-hosting asks you to connect your own sources — in exchange for never handing the index of your company’s knowledge to anyone. For a sensitive corpus, that’s the trade worth making.

The bottom line

The best Glean alternative in 2026 gives your team the same superpower — ask anything, get an answer sourced from your own documents — without shipping the company’s entire knowledge base to a vendor. Self-hosted retrieval, local models, memory that persists, and answers that turn into action, all on hardware you control. You keep the intelligence and keep the data. For anything sensitive, high-stakes, or regulated, that’s not a preference — it’s the requirement.
