Q4 Content Calendar With AI — September to New Year, Planned Once

MARKETINGAUGUST 19, 20265 MIN READ

Q4 content fails the same way every year: strong September, thinning October, and by Black Friday week — the exact moment attention peaks — the feed goes quiet because the person running it is buried in orders. Building a Q4 content calendar with AI solves the right problem: not "what should I post" but "how does posting continue when I have zero spare minutes." The answer is planning once, producing in batches, and letting the calendar run itself while you run the business.

The shape of the season

Q4 isn't one push — it's four arcs. September is warm-up: useful, unsalesy content that rebuilds reach after summer. October is seasonal play: Halloween creative (your low-risk rehearsal — see AI Halloween marketing assets) plus early gift-thinking content. November is the offer arc: teasers, the Black Friday push, and daily presence through the peak weekend. December splits in two — gifting urgency until your last shipping date, then a deliberate wind-down: year-in-review, thank-you posts, and January seeds. Sketch those four arcs first; dates and posts hang off them.

Plan the skeleton with AI in one session

Give your AI assistant the fixed dates — Black Friday, Cyber Monday, your last shipping day, any launch — plus your channels and honest capacity ("three posts a week, one email"). Ask for the full-week skeleton from September through December: each slot with a topic, format, and one-line angle. You'll edit a third of it; that's fine. The value is that a specific mediocre plan gets improved, while a blank calendar gets abandoned.

Plan capacity for November-you, not August-you. August-you feels ambitious. November-you is packing boxes at midnight. Every November slot should be fillable from pre-made assets in under five minutes.

Batch production weeks

Schedule two production weeks — mid-September and mid-October — and produce a month ahead in each. That's when you generate the visuals: seasonal scenes from the AI product photo generator, short spots from the video generator (formats in AI holiday video ads), and captions in bulk from the caption generator. Email gets drafted in the same batches — the arcs are in AI holiday email campaigns. Load everything into your scheduler the same week. From then on the calendar is edited, not fed.

Leave 20% empty

A fully scheduled calendar has no room for the real thing that happens — the product that sells out, the order photo a customer posts, the shipping delay you need to address. Keep roughly one slot in five unplanned for reactive posts. The scheduled 80% keeps you alive; the reactive 20% is usually what performs best, because it's the part that's unmistakably human.

Frequently asked questions

How many channels can one person run in Q4? With batching, two well and four adequately — one primary social channel, email, and syndicated variants elsewhere. Email is the one you protect if forced to cut.

Should posting frequency increase in Q4? Around your offer windows, yes — daily through Black Friday week. The rest of the season, consistency beats volume.

What if the plan stops matching reality? Edit the next two weeks and leave the rest. The calendar is a default, not a contract.
