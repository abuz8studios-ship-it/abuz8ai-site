What a board deck is supposed to do

A good board deck does three things: it tells your directors how the business is actually doing without spin, it surfaces the two or three decisions you need their help on, and it does both fast enough that the meeting is a discussion, not a recital. Most first-time founders over-build the recital and under-build the asks. The structure matters because it forces the right shape.

The mistake first-time founders make

The classic first-time error is treating the board meeting like a performance review you have to pass. The deck becomes forty slides of everything that went right, the misses get buried or spun, and the asks — the entire reason you have a board — get one rushed slide at the end when everyone's already tired.

Experienced founders invert it. They state the misses early and plainly, because a board that trusts your bad news will believe your good news. They keep the recital tight so the room has time to actually help. And they make the asks specific: not "we'd love intros," but "we need a warm intro to two VPs of Engineering at Series-C fintechs by month-end." The structure the generator produces nudges you toward that shape on purpose — it puts the asks where they can't be skipped, because a deck that hides them wastes the most expensive meeting on your calendar.

Three numbers your directors look for first

Whatever else is on the slides, experienced board members scan for three things before anything else:

  • Runway. How many months of cash at current burn. It frames every other decision in the room, so put it where nobody has to hunt for it.
  • Growth rate, not just the absolute. "Up and to the right" is table stakes; the slope — and whether it's accelerating — is the actual story.
  • Net retention. Whether existing customers expand or churn tells directors more about the health of the business than any new-logo count.

The generator surfaces all three near the top by default — because a board deck that buries them reads as a deck trying to hide something, and directors notice.
