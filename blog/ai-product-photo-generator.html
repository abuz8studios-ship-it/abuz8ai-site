Why product photos are the whole game for sellers

On every marketplace and storefront, the photo is the product until it arrives at the door. A muddy, badly lit snapshot tanks conversion no matter how good the thing actually is. But proper product photography is slow and expensive — and for a small seller with fifty SKUs, repeating it every time you add inventory is a tax you can't afford. The generator removes that tax: snapshot in, catalog-ready image out.

Under the hood it isolates your product from its messy background, then relights and re-stages it in a generated scene while keeping the actual object — its shape, label, color, texture — intact. That last part is the constraint that separates a usable tool from a toy: the background can be invented, but the product cannot drift.

How to get catalog-ready results

Shoot the input flat and in focus

The model can replace the background but it can't sharpen a blurry product. Lay the item down, get the whole thing in frame, tap to focus, and use daylight from a window. A crisp input is 80% of a great output.

Keep the background plain when you shoot

A cluttered input makes it harder to cut the product out cleanly. A plain surface — a sheet of paper, a clean table — gives the model an easy edge to work with, so the cutout looks natural instead of haloed.

Generate a set, not a single

Make a white-background shot for the marketplace's main image, a lifestyle scene for your ads, and a close-up for the detail slot. A consistent set across all your SKUs reads as a real brand; one-off looks read as improvised.

The honesty pass

What it does well: turns home snapshots into clean, consistent, on-background shots fast and free — a genuine unlock for anyone selling without a studio. What it doesn't do: it won't perfectly render fine print on a label, complex reflective surfaces (think mirrored chrome), or transparent glass without occasional artifacts. For those, check the output at full size and reshoot the input if a label has to be readable.

If you're shooting a luxury watch campaign, hire the photographer. If you need fifty clean marketplace shots this week, this is the road.
