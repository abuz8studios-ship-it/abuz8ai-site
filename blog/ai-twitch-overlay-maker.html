Overlay packs on Etsy and Gumroad run $20–150, and every one of them is on a thousand other channels. An AI stream overlay maker workflow gets you a kit nobody else has, matched to your emotes and your colors, for the cost of an evening. Here's the full scene list and how to generate each piece so OBS assembles into a channel that looks designed rather than downloaded.

The five scenes every channel needs

Starting soon (1920x1080, with a clear space for a countdown), in-game overlay (webcam frame plus a slim alert bar — the game is the content, so less is more), just-chatting frame (fuller decoration, room for chat), be-right-back, and offline/ending screen with your schedule and socials. That's the whole kit. Generate them as one batch with one style block so they read as a set.

Generate backgrounds, not layouts

The mistake is asking AI to design the layout — text baked into images ages badly and can't be edited. Instead, generate atmospheric backgrounds in your channel style (same palette and shading language as your emotes), then add text, timers and frames as OBS text sources and browser sources on top. When your schedule changes, you edit a text field, not a PNG. Panel headers and webcam frames are the exception — generate those as transparent PNGs with a background remover pass.

Readability rule: your in-game overlay competes with gameplay for attention and must lose. Keep it under 10% of screen area, high contrast only at the webcam frame, and test it over the busiest game you play — an overlay that works over a menu screen and vanishes over a firefight is a failed overlay.

Match the whole surface area

The kit extends past OBS: panel images for your about/schedule/donate sections (320px wide), a profile banner, thumbnails for VODs and clips going to YouTube, and social headers. Use your locked style descriptors for all of it — if you built a mascot with a cartoon avatar tool, it should appear across every surface. Viewers can't articulate why a channel feels professional; the answer is always repetition of one visual language.

Animated later, static now

Static kits convert fine; animation is polish, not foundation. When you're ready, the highest-impact upgrades in order: a subtle loop on the starting-soon background, an animated webcam frame, alert animations. Add motion to one scene at a time — a fully animated kit on a laggy encode is worse than a clean static one.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution should stream overlays be? Design at 1920x1080. OBS scales down cleanly for 720p output but can't invent detail scaling up.

Should text be part of the overlay image? No — keep text as OBS text sources over generated backgrounds so schedules and titles stay editable without regenerating art.

Can I sell AI overlay kits? Generally yes under most generators' licenses — overlay packs are a real micro-business. Keep styles original and check your tool's terms.
