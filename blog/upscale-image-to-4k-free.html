The model doing the heavy lifting

The studio runs 4x-UltraSharp, a widely respected super-resolution model that multiplies each dimension by four. A 1000-pixel-wide photo comes back around 4000 pixels wide — genuine 4K-class output, not a stretched bitmap. Unlike bicubic resizing, which invents nothing and blurs everything, a trained upscaler reconstructs plausible detail: text edges stay crisp, skin stays skin-like, brick stays brick. It is the difference between a bigger file and a bigger photograph.

Why local upscaling beats the upload sites

Three reasons, in descending order of how often they bite people. First, resolution caps: hosted upscalers throttle output size because big renders cost them money — on your own GPU the cap is your VRAM. Second, privacy: family photos, client scans, and unreleased product shots do not belong in a stranger's processing queue, a point we make at length in local AI vs. cloud AI. Third, batch economics: if you have four hundred old photos to restore, credits multiply and local compute does not.

Print check: at 300 DPI, a 4000-pixel-wide image prints cleanly at about 13 inches wide. That is the threshold where an old phone photo becomes a frameable print. If print output is the goal, our companion piece on upscaling for print covers sizing math and paper choices.

What it will not do

Honesty section. An upscaler cannot recover information that was never captured: a face that is eight pixels wide will come back sharper, not identifiable. Heavy JPEG artifacts upscale into sharper artifacts unless you clean them first. And a 4x model quadruples dimensions — it does not colorize, restore torn edges, or fix focus. Frequently asked questions

How big can the output actually get? The model multiplies each dimension by four, and the practical ceiling is your graphics card's memory rather than a service plan. Most mid-range cards handle a 4x pass on a typical phone photo comfortably; very large sources may need to be processed in stages.

Will it fix blur or JPEG compression? Not by itself. Super-resolution sharpens and enlarges what is there; it does not refocus a missed shot, and heavy compression artifacts will enlarge along with the detail. Clean the source first when you can — the upscaler rewards good input disproportionately, and a two-minute crop-and-denoise pass before upscaling routinely improves the result more than any setting change after it.

Why does the first run take longer than the rest? The engine loads the model into GPU memory on first use. That one-time load can take a while; every run after it in the same session skips the load and returns in seconds. Leave the engine running while you batch.
