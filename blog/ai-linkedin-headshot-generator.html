Why the LinkedIn photo is its own problem

A LinkedIn headshot isn't art. It's a one-second trust test. A recruiter scanning forty profiles spends roughly that long on your photo before deciding whether to read your headline. The job of the image is narrow: look approachable, look competent, look like someone who shows up. That's it. You don't need editorial lighting — you need a clean, well-lit, head-on shot that reads "professional" at thumbnail size.

That narrowness is exactly why AI does it so well. The model isn't trying to win a photography prize. It's hitting a known, well-defined target that has been photographed ten million times.

How to get a profile photo that converts

Shoot the input near a window

Soft daylight from the side is the most flattering free lighting on earth. The model copies whatever light it sees on your face, so a clean input becomes a clean set of outputs. Overhead office fluorescents do you no favors — they carve shadows under the eyes that every render inherits.

Look straight at the lens

Three-quarter angles can work, but a head-on shot gives the model the most geometry to lock onto, and head-on reads as the most trustworthy at small sizes. Trust is the whole point of a LinkedIn photo.

Pick the wardrobe to match the room you want to be in

Applying for a finance role? Use the boardroom preset. Pitching design clients? The creative-director preset. The tool ships tested prompt stacks for each, and the prompt field stays open if you want "navy blazer, soft gray studio background" exactly.

Is it safe to use an AI headshot for a real job hunt?

Reasonable question, and we wrote a whole honest breakdown of it in are AI headshots safe. Short version: a headshot is a representation, not a document. As long as the output genuinely looks like you on a normal day — same face, same general grooming — it's no more "fake" than a photographer's retouching pass. Where people get into trouble is using a render that flatters them into a different person. Keep it honest and you keep it useful.
