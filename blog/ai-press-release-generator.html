AI Press Release Generator: Write One a Journalist Won't Delete

AI WRITINGJUNE 19, 20266 MIN READ

An AI press release generator can produce a clean, correctly formatted release in under a minute — dateline, boilerplate, quote, the works. What it can't do is make your news actually newsworthy, and that's the part that decides whether a journalist reads past the headline or sends it to trash. Used well, AI handles the formatting drudgery so you can focus on the angle. Used as a hype machine, it writes the exact release reporters have trained themselves to ignore. Here's the line.

Format is solved — your angle isn't

The genuine value of an AI press release generator is that it nails the boring, rigid structure every time: the inverted pyramid, the location dateline, the third-person voice, the boilerplate at the bottom, the "###" end mark. That's worth real time. But formatting was never why releases get ignored. They get ignored because the "news" is a company talking about itself. Before you generate a single word, answer one question: why would a stranger care? If the only answer is "we launched a thing," the polish won't save it. AI writes the wrapper; you have to bring the news.

Front-load the one fact that matters

Journalists read the first sentence and decide. So the headline and opening line must carry the actual news — what happened, why it's notable — not a windup. "Company announces exciting new initiative to better serve customers" says nothing; "Company X cut customer wait times 70% with a tool it's giving away free" says something. Prompt the generator with the single most concrete, surprising fact and tell it to lead with it. AI defaults to a warm-up sentence; make it skip the throat-clearing and open on the news.

The hype words that get you deleted: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "cutting-edge," "world-class," "leading provider," "next-generation," "synergy," "disruptive." Reporters see these as a signal that there's no real story underneath. An AI generator sprinkles them in automatically because press releases in its training data are full of them. Cut every one and replace it with a number, a name, or a fact. A release that says "3,000 users in 30 days" beats one that says "explosive growth" — specificity reads as truth; superlatives read as cover.

The quote should sound like a person said it

Every release has a CEO quote, and almost every one is dead on arrival: "We're thrilled to deliver innovative solutions that empower our customers." Nobody talks like that, and reporters never run it. A usable quote says something a human would actually say — an opinion, a reason, a bit of edge. Tell the AI to write the quote in a real speaking voice and to make a point, not a platitude. Then read it aloud; if it sounds like a robot in a suit, rewrite it. The quote is your one chance to inject a personality into an otherwise neutral document.

Pair it with the rest of your announcement kit

A press release rarely travels alone. It goes out with a pitch email, lands on your blog, and gets cut into social posts. Generate them as a set from the same facts so the story stays consistent across surfaces — the release for the wire, a shorter human pitch for the reporter, a post for your own site. One news event, several tailored shapes, beats one release blasted everywhere.

When NOT to send a press release at all

Here's the honest part most "AI PR" tools won't tell you: for an early-stage company, a press release is often the wrong tool. Wire services cost money, most releases get zero pickup, and a personal email to one relevant reporter beats a blast to a thousand. Use a formal release when you have genuine news and a distribution channel that fits — a funding round, a real milestone, a regulatory event. For "we shipped a feature," a good post and a few direct messages outperform a release every time. Don't let an easy generator talk you into a channel your news doesn't justify.

The bottom line

An AI press release generator is a great formatter and a dangerous hype machine. Let it handle the rigid structure, but bring the actual news yourself: lead with the one concrete fact, write a quote a human would say, cut every superlative, and produce the release as part of a matched kit. Most importantly, be honest about whether you need a release at all — sometimes the highest-ROI PR move is one well-aimed email the generator never sees.
