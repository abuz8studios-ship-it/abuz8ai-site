Marketing: words that pull their weight

The AI ad copy generator earns its slot first. Feed it your offer, audience, and platform; get back headline and body variants you can A/B instead of staring at a blinking cursor at 11 p.m. The trick with any copy generator: give it your actual customer language — the phrases from your reviews and DMs — and edit the output like a ruthless customer would read it. AI drafts; you decide. That division of labor is the whole game.

Product images without the studio invoice

Two tools cover most visual jobs a small shop has. The AI background remover turns phone photos into clean product shots — white-background listings, marketplace thumbnails, the works. Then the image compressor makes those images web-weight so your product pages load before your customer's patience runs out. Page speed is a sales variable, not a nerd metric: heavy images are the most common self-inflicted wound on small-business sites, and the fix costs nothing and takes seconds.

Boardroom documents, minus the boardroom

The consulting-grade shelf is where the collection gets unusual. There's a SWOT analyzer for the annual gut-check, a pricing strategy builder when you suspect you've been undercharging since 2023 (you probably have), and an OKR generator if your "goals" are currently a notes-app poem. These produce structured drafts a consultant would charge four figures to format. You still supply the judgment — the tools supply the structure and the blank-page escape velocity.

If you're already paying for AI

Plenty of small businesses now carry an OpenAI or Anthropic line item without anyone having done the arithmetic. Fifteen minutes fixes that: the token counter shows what your prompts cost per call, and the prompt caching calculator models the discount you're probably not claiming on repeated prompts. If you're automating anything at volume — support drafts, product descriptions, order emails — those two pages routinely find real money. Measure before you optimize; it's cheaper.

The one-afternoon rollout

Here's the order that gets results fastest, assuming one free afternoon. Hour one: images — run your top ten product photos through background removal and compression, re-upload, and feel your pages get faster the same day. Hour two: copy — regenerate your three worst-performing ads or listings with the ad-copy tool, edit hard, and ship them as A/B tests against the originals. Hour three: the paperwork you've been dodging — a SWOT draft and a pricing-strategy pass, printed, scribbled on, argued with over coffee. If you have an AI subscription, spend the last thirty minutes on the token counter and caching calculator; that half hour has the highest expected dollar value of the whole afternoon. One afternoon, zero spend, and every artifact is something you'd otherwise have paid for or postponed.

The limits, stated plainly

Free tools won't run your business unattended. They draft, convert, compress, and structure; they don't know your margins, your customers, or your taste. Budget your own review time for anything customer-facing — an unedited AI paragraph reads like one. And keep your sensitive numbers out of random web tools generally; the browser utilities here run client-side, but the principle stands everywhere: know where your data goes before you paste it.
