This week: how AI agents actually work in production.

Hey {{first_name|default:"builder"}} — most "AI agent" content is demos and hype. This week I wrote down the unglamorous stuff that decides whether an agent survives contact with the real world: deployment, cost, grounding, and where the whole thing lives. Seven new pieces, all from things that broke (or worked) while building QADIR OS.

New on the blog
How to Deploy an AI Agent
The real checklist. The easy 10% is pushing code; the hard 90% is permissions, memory, monitoring, and a kill switch you've actually tested.
AI Model Routing Explained
Send each task to the cheapest model that can do it. Local for the routine 90%, frontier for the hard 10%. The biggest lever on agent cost — and almost nobody uses it.
RAG Explained (No Jargon)
The open-book exam that stops your agent confidently making things up about your business. Chunk, embed, retrieve, generate — in plain English.
The Agentic Loop Explained
The one idea that separates a real agent from a chatbot: perceive, plan, act, observe, repeat — until the job is actually done.
Self-Improving AI Agents: Real vs. Hype
They don't get smarter — they get wiser. And the experience only compounds for you if you own the memory it's built on.
Run AI Agents on an RTX 5090
Local models, local memory, local video — on one machine, near-zero running cost, nothing leaving your desk.
AI Agents for Lead Generation
What works (research, personalization, follow-up) and the spam trap that gets your domain blacklisted. Patience beats volume.
The thread running through all of it

The hype is always in what an agent can do. The value is in the constraints — what it's allowed to do, what it costs to run, where its memory lives, how you stop it. Capabilities make the demo. Constraints make it trustable.
