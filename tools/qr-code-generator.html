Generate a QR code for anything, in seconds

A QR code is just a compact way to hand a phone a piece of information — a link, a WiFi login, a phone number — without anyone typing it. Point a camera at the square, and the phone does the rest. PNG or SVG — which download?

PNG is a ready-made image at the pixel size you choose (256 to 2048 px). It is the fast option for social posts, slides, emails and documents. SVG is vector: it is drawn from shapes, not pixels, so it stays razor-sharp at any size — from a business card to a shop window to a billboard. If the code will be printed, especially large, choose SVG. Both encode exactly the same data, so a scanner reads them identically.

Error correction, explained plainly

QR codes carry spare data so they still scan when part of the pattern is smudged, torn or covered. That is the error-correction level. Higher levels survive more damage but pack the pattern denser:

H~30%rough conditions or a logo dropped in the center

Tips for codes that always scan

Keep strong contrast — a dark foreground on a light background. Avoid inverting to light-on-dark; many scanners struggle with it. Leave the quiet zone (the empty margin around the code) intact — this tool adds it for you, so do not crop it out. Print at a sensible size: as a rule of thumb, a code should be at least 2 cm square for close scanning and larger for distance. And shorter data makes a simpler, more forgiving pattern, so trim long URLs where you can.

Is my data private?

Yes. The whole QR code is generated by a self-contained encoder running as plain JavaScript inside your browser — there is no server call, no upload, and nothing is logged or stored. Your link, WiFi password or contact details never leave your device. Refresh the page and everything is gone, which makes this safe for private and internal use.
