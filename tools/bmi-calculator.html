How to calculate BMI

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a single number that relates your weight to your height. It is the most widely used quick screen for whether an adult's weight sits in a broadly healthy range for their height. The calculator above works in either unit system — type your numbers and the BMI, category and healthy-weight range update instantly.

The formula

UnitsFormulaExample
Metricweight(kg) / height(m)²70 / (1.75)² = 22.9
Imperial703 × weight(lb) / height(in)²703 × 154 / 69² = 22.7

Both formulas describe the same thing — the imperial version just folds in the 703 factor that converts pounds-and-inches into the metric result. This tool computes everything in metric internally and converts your imperial input for you, so the two agree.

BMI categories (adults)

These are the standard World Health Organization ranges for adults. They are general population thresholds and are not adjusted for age, sex, muscle mass or ethnicity.

BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Healthy weight
25.0 – 29.9Overweight
30.0 and aboveObesity

What BMI does not tell you

BMI is a screen, not a verdict. Because it uses only height and weight, it cannot tell muscle from fat — a lean, muscular athlete can read as "overweight," while someone with low muscle can read "healthy" while carrying excess fat. It is also less reliable for children and teenagers (who use age- and sex-specific percentiles instead), for older adults, during pregnancy, and across some ethnic groups where risk thresholds differ. Waist measurement, body-composition testing and a conversation with a healthcare professional give a fuller picture. Use your BMI as one useful data point, not the whole story.
